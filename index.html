a little about me

From the Taj Mahal's air to Himalayan snow

My research career began at Dayalbagh Educational Institute, Agra, studying the chemistry and meteorology of air pollution around the Taj Mahal. Over three decades it has grown into an inter- and trans-disciplinary programme covering climate change, agricultural reactive nitrogen, plants and air pollution, atmospheric mercury and the long-range transport of pollutants across South Asia.

Before joining JNU as Professor in 2008, I spent ten years as a scientist at CSIR–Indian Institute of Chemical Technology, Hyderabad, after postdoctoral work at CSIR–National Physical Laboratory, New Delhi, and the Department of Meteorology at Stockholm University.

I edit Current World Environment as Editor-in-Chief, serve on the boards of Aerosol and Air Quality Research and the Journal of the Indian Geophysical Union, and was an Expert Reviewer for IPCC AR5 (Working Group I).

Our group's research has real socio-economic and geo-political weight for the Indian region, because clean air is never just a local question.

First to report

Acid rain over the Indian Ocean

Winter rain over the Indian Ocean turned out to be commonly acidic, driven by low calcium and excess non-sea-salt sulphate. That's the reverse of what happens over the Indian mainland.

Dust as a buffer

Calcium carbonate in dust neutralises acidity over continental India.

Chlorine and ozone

Banning plastic burning cut chlorine emissions, and urban ozone spiked as less of it got destroyed. Followed up in 2025 with the role of HCl and Cl₂ in Delhi's ozone.

How the Indo-Gangetic Plain breathes

Valley topography and a "camel-ride push" of winter winds flush NCR pollution toward the Bay of Bengal in about 8 to 10 days. I've also proposed artificial lakes as a way to tame Delhi's dust.

Urban dust and plants

City dust measurably changes the biochemistry of plant leaves.

Reactive nitrogen and land use

Links poor air quality to aerosol and nitrogen emissions from land-use change and long-range transport.
